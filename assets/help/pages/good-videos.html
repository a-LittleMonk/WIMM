Recording videos that track well

Most tracking problems are decided when the video is recorded. These rules make detection reliable and the numbers comparable between animals.

Camera

  • Above the arena, looking straight down. A tilted camera makes the far side look smaller, so distances there are too short. If you can't avoid an angle, use Correct perspective (Arena).
  • Fixed. The camera must not move during the session or between animals of one batch. The background model assumes a still camera.
  • The whole arena in the picture, with a little margin, filling as much of the frame as possible.
  • Same camera position, zoom and lighting for every animal of an experiment.

Contrast between mouse and floor

  • Black mouse on a white or light floor, or white mouse on a dark floor, tracks best.
  • A white mouse on a white floor (or black on black) needs White mouse on white floor in Detection and careful checking.
  • Even, diffuse light. Avoid strong shadows, reflections on the floor or walls and lamps visible in the picture. Infrared lighting works well for dark-phase tests.

Resolution, frame rate, format

  • 720p (1280 × 720) is plenty; 1080p works but is slower. Lower resolutions work if the mouse is at least about 20 × 20 pixels.
  • 25–30 frames per second is normal. The app reads each frame's real time stamp, so small dropped-frame gaps are handled correctly.
  • Formats: .mp4 .avi .mov .mkv .wmv .flv. If a file reports an impossible frame rate (for example 1000 fps) and has no time stamps, the app asks you to set the FPS override (Video).

The empty arena

The app builds a picture of the empty arena (the "background") from frames spread over the video, and finds the mouse as what differs from it. This works even if the mouse is present from the start — but objects that are moved during the session, a hand that stays in the picture, or a changing light make it harder. Start the recording a few seconds before the mouse goes in if you can, and trim that part off (Trim).

Before a full study

Record one or two pilot animals, run them, and check the tracking issues and the detection rate (aim for 90 % or more). Fix the set-up now — it is much easier than correcting hundreds of videos later.
